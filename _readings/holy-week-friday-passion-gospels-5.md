<p><span class="v">3</span>Then Judas, who betrayed him, when he saw that Jesus was condemned, felt remorse, and brought back the thirty pieces of silver to the chief priests and elders,  <span class="v">4</span>saying, “I have sinned in that I betrayed innocent blood.” But they said, “What is that to us? You see to it.”  <span class="v">5</span>He threw down the pieces of silver in the sanctuary, and departed. He went away and hanged himself.  <span class="v">6</span>The chief priests took the pieces of silver, and said, “It’s not lawful to put them into the treasury, since it is the price of blood.”  <span class="v">7</span>They took counsel, and bought the potter’s field with them, to bury strangers in.  <span class="v">8</span>Therefore that field was called “The Field of Blood” to this day.  <span class="v">9</span>Then that which was spoken through Jeremiah the prophet was fulfilled, saying, “They took the thirty pieces of silver, the price of him upon whom a price had been set, whom some of the children of Israel priced,  <span class="v">10</span>and they gave them for the potter’s field, as the Lord commanded me.”  <span class="v">11</span>Now Jesus stood before the governor: and the governor asked him, saying, “Are you the King of the Jews?” Jesus said to him, “So you say.”  <span class="v">12</span>When he was accused by the chief priests and elders, he answered nothing.  <span class="v">13</span>Then Pilate said to him, “Don’t you hear how many things they testify against you?”  <span class="v">14</span>He gave him no answer, not even one word, so that the governor marveled greatly.  <span class="v">15</span>Now at the feast the governor was accustomed to release to the multitude one prisoner, whom they desired.  <span class="v">16</span>They had then a notable prisoner, called Barabbas.  <span class="v">17</span>When therefore they were gathered together, Pilate said to them, “Whom do you want me to release to you? Barabbas, or Jesus, who is called Christ?”  <span class="v">18</span>For he knew that because of envy they had delivered him up.  <span class="v">19</span>While he was sitting on the judgment seat, his wife sent to him, saying, “Have nothing to do with that righteous man, for I have suffered many things today in a dream because of him.”  <span class="v">20</span>Now the chief priests and the elders persuaded the multitudes to ask for Barabbas, and destroy Jesus.  <span class="v">21</span>But the governor answered them, “Which of the two do you want me to release to you?” They said, “Barabbas!”  <span class="v">22</span>Pilate said to them, “What then shall I do to Jesus, who is called Christ?” They all said to him, “Let him be crucified!”  <span class="v">23</span>But the governor said, “Why? What evil has he done?” But they cried out exceedingly, saying, “Let him be crucified!”  <span class="v">24</span>So when Pilate saw that nothing was being gained, but rather that a disturbance was starting, he took water, and washed his hands before the multitude, saying, “I am innocent of the blood of this righteous person. You see to it.”  <span class="v">25</span>All the people answered, “May his blood be on us, and on our children!”  <span class="v">26</span>Then he released to them Barabbas, but Jesus he flogged and delivered to be crucified.  <span class="v">27</span>Then the governor’s soldiers took Jesus into the Praetorium, and gathered the whole garrison together against him.  <span class="v">28</span>They stripped him, and put a scarlet robe on him.  <span class="v">29</span>They braided a crown of thorns and put it on his head, and a reed in his right hand; and they kneeled down before him, and mocked him, saying, “Hail, King of the Jews!”  <span class="v">30</span>They spat on him, and took the reed and struck him on the head.  <span class="v">31</span>When they had mocked him, they took the robe off him, and put his clothes on him, and led him away to crucify him.  <span class="v">32</span>As they came out, they found a man of Cyrene, Simon by name, and they compelled him to go with them, that he might carry his cross. </p>
