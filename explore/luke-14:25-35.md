<div class="verse"><span>25</span>Now great multitudes were going with him. He turned and said to them, </div> <div class="verse"><span>26</span>“If anyone comes to me, and doesn’t disregard his own father, mother, wife, children, brothers, and sisters, yes, and his own life also, he can’t be my disciple. </div> <div class="verse"><span>27</span>Whoever doesn’t bear his own cross, and come after me, can’t be my disciple. </div> <div class="verse"><span>28</span>For which of you, desiring to build a tower, doesn’t first sit down and count the cost, to see if he has enough to complete it? </div> <div class="verse"><span>29</span>Or perhaps, when he has laid a foundation, and is not able to finish, everyone who sees begins to mock him, </div> <div class="verse"><span>30</span>saying, ‘This man began to build, and wasn’t able to finish.’ </div> <div class="verse"><span>31</span>Or what king, as he goes to encounter another king in war, will not sit down first and consider whether he is able with ten thousand to meet him who comes against him with twenty thousand? </div> <div class="verse"><span>32</span>Or else, while the other is yet a great way off, he sends an envoy, and asks for conditions of peace. </div> <div class="verse"><span>33</span>So therefore whoever of you who doesn’t renounce all that he has, he can’t be my disciple. </div> <div class="verse"><span>34</span>Salt is good, but if the salt becomes flat and tasteless, with what do you season it? </div> <div class="verse"><span>35</span>It is fit neither for the soil nor for the manure pile. It is thrown out. He who has ears to hear, let him hear.” </div>
