<div class="verse"><span>9</span>He began to tell the people this parable. “A man planted a vineyard, and rented it out to some farmers, and went into another country for a long time. </div> <div class="verse"><span>10</span>At the proper season, he sent a servant to the farmers to collect his share of the fruit of the vineyard. But the farmers beat him, and sent him away empty. </div> <div class="verse"><span>11</span>He sent yet another servant, and they also beat him, and treated him shamefully, and sent him away empty. </div> <div class="verse"><span>12</span>He sent yet a third, and they also wounded him, and threw him out. </div> <div class="verse"><span>13</span>The lord of the vineyard said, ‘What shall I do? I will send my beloved son. It may be that seeing him, they will respect him.’ </div> <div class="verse"><span>14</span>“But when the farmers saw him, they reasoned among themselves, saying, ‘This is the heir. Come, let’s kill him, that the inheritance may be ours.’ </div> <div class="verse"><span>15</span>They threw him out of the vineyard, and killed him. What therefore will the lord of the vineyard do to them? </div> <div class="verse"><span>16</span>He will come and destroy these farmers, and will give the vineyard to others.” When they heard it, they said, “May it never be!” </div> <div class="verse"><span>17</span>But he looked at them, and said, “Then what is this that is written, ‘The stone which the builders rejected, the same was made the chief cornerstone?’ </div> <div class="verse"><span>18</span>Everyone who falls on that stone will be broken to pieces, but it will crush whomever it falls on to dust.” </div>
