<div class="verse"><span>1</span>Now on the second Sabbath after the first, he was going through the grain fields. His disciples plucked the heads of grain and ate, rubbing them in their hands. </div> <div class="verse"><span>2</span>But some of the Pharisees said to them, “Why do you do that which is not lawful to do on the Sabbath day?” </div> <div class="verse"><span>3</span>Jesus, answering them, said, “Haven’t you read what David did when he was hungry, he, and those who were with him; </div> <div class="verse"><span>4</span>how he entered into God’s house, and took and ate the show bread, and gave also to those who were with him, which is not lawful to eat except for the priests alone?” </div> <div class="verse"><span>5</span>He said to them, “The Son of Man is lord of the Sabbath.” </div> <div class="verse"><span>6</span>It also happened on another Sabbath that he entered into the synagogue and taught. There was a man there, and his right hand was withered. </div> <div class="verse"><span>7</span>The scribes and the Pharisees watched him, to see whether he would heal on the Sabbath, that they might find an accusation against him. </div> <div class="verse"><span>8</span>But he knew their thoughts; and he said to the man who had the withered hand, “Rise up, and stand in the middle.” He arose and stood. </div> <div class="verse"><span>9</span>Then Jesus said to them, “I will ask you something: Is it lawful on the Sabbath to do good, or to do harm? To save a life, or to kill?” </div> <div class="verse"><span>10</span>He looked around at them all, and said to the man, “Stretch out your hand.” He did, and his hand was restored as sound as the other. </div> <div class="verse"><span>11</span>But they were filled with rage, and talked with one another about what they might do to Jesus. </div>
